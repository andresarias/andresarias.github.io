Bio

Corporate depth. Academic rigor. Human warmth.

I have spent more than two decades inside demanding technology organizations, most recently as a senior customer solutions leader at Amazon Web Services, guiding large enterprises across the Americas through cloud and AI transformation.

Along the way I earned a doctorate in business administration at Florida International University, where I also teach: business intelligence, analytics, statistics, and AI strategy, for undergraduates, graduate students, and executives.

The through-line in all of it is the same. I help people at inflection points, the moments when the old chapter has clearly ended and the new one has not yet taken shape, see themselves more clearly and move.

Jobs are bundles of tasks. AI is unbundling them. The question that matters is how we rebundle what remains.

Research

The human cost of technology adoption.

My doctoral research at Florida International University examines the overlooked human side of enterprise technology adoption: why it produces burnout and turnover, and what makes some professionals resilient to it.

The work identifies product management acumen, the ability to think in outcomes, systems, and user value rather than tasks, as a measurable protective factor. It is the research foundation behind my teaching, advising, and writing on AI and the future of work.

Why does technology adoption burn people out, and what separates the people who thrive from the ones who quit?
